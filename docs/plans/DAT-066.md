## Objective

The store can hold both of two regions of one country that have one
name, as the standard's own list has them, when their codes tell them
apart. Of the regions of a country that say no code, no two have one
name.

## Why

Since DAT-013 no two regions of one country have one name. The list of
ISO 3166-2 itself has regions that do: on the list that comes with
release 4.20.1 of iso-codes the planner counted 36 pairs of regions
that share a name within one country, as Maputo, the city (`MZ-MPM`)
and the province (`MZ-L`) of Mozambique. Of each such pair the store
can hold one today, and whoever loads the regions of a country would
have to leave the other out or give it a name its source does not
give it. The project invents no value (`CLAUDE.md` §11). Since DAT-064
a region can say its code, and since DAT-067 the code is held to the
country: the code tells the two apart.

## What this task is, and what it is not

- **It is** the rule of the names written anew, in the model `Region`
  and by a twentieth migration that builds the table of the regions
  once more; an index over the country, which the old rule's index
  was; their tests; and the documents.
- **It is not** a new column, and no row changes: every region keeps
  all it had, its key and its code.
- **It is not** a judgement of which regions are one. A region that
  says its code and one of its name that says none are both stored.
  Whether they are one region is a question for a person, asked by the
  task that first stores regions (see "What the trial measured and
  settled").
- **It is not** the loading of regions, and not the standard's list:
  the store has no regions, and nothing stores one yet.
- **It is not** a region inside a region (DAT-065).
- **It is not** a change to a command, or to the code that stores and
  changes records.

## Design in short

The rule that was written into the table,

    CONSTRAINT uq_region_country_id_name UNIQUE (country_id, name)

goes, and an index that holds the same of fewer rows takes its place:

    CREATE UNIQUE INDEX ix_region_country_id_name ON region (country_id, name)
    WHERE iso_3166_2 IS NULL

In the model that is, in `__table_args__` of `Region`,

    Index(
        None,
        "country_id",
        "name",
        unique=True,
        sqlite_where=text("iso_3166_2 IS NULL"),
    ),

where `None` leaves the name to the project's rule of names
(`ix_region_country_id_name`). The country gets an index of its own,

    country_id: Mapped[int] = mapped_column(ForeignKey("country.id"), index=True)

which is `CREATE INDEX ix_region_country_id ON region (country_id)`.

SQLite cannot take a rule out of a table that is there. So the
twentieth migration, `let_regions_with_codes_share_a_name`, builds the
table anew, as the seventeenth did: it copies the rows and the highest
key aside, drops the table, makes it again without the one rule, fills
it from the copy with every column and every key, puts the highest key
back, drops the copies, and makes again what went with the table: the
index over the sources (`ix_region_source_id`), then the two new
indexes, then the two triggers of the nineteenth migration that are on
the regions.

## What the trial measured and settled

- **What the standard's list has.** In
  `data/iso_3166-2.json` of release 4.20.1 of iso-codes: 5046 codes of
  200 countries; 36 pairs of regions with one name within one country,
  72 codes; no name three times in a country; the most regions of one
  country are the 221 of the United Kingdom. The four pairs the new
  tests use are among the 36, each with its two codes: Maputo
  (`MZ-L`, `MZ-MPM`), Tartu (`EE-793`, `EE-796`), Cantabria (`ES-CB`,
  `ES-S`) and Dhaka (`BD-13`, `BD-C`).
- **The rule holds among the regions that say no code.** Two regions
  of a country that both say a code can have one name: no two regions
  have one code (DAT-064), so the codes tell them apart. Two that say
  none cannot: nothing would. That is one index over the regions
  without a code, and SQLite says of a refusal what it said of the
  old rule, `UNIQUE constraint failed: region.country_id, region.name`,
  so whoever reads the refusal reads the same words.
- **A region with a code and one without can have one name.** The
  table takes both. They may be one region, which one source names
  with its code and another without, or two parts of the country, as
  the city and the province of Maputo are: the table cannot know, and
  a rule that refused the second would keep a true region out as often
  as a double one. `CLAUDE.md` §11 says where the question goes: "Do
  not automatically merge uncertain rider or track identities. Use
  review queues when information conflicts." The task that first
  stores regions asks it in the review queue (`DATABASE.md`, "Opening
  a possible duplicate"); nothing stores a region yet, and this task
  writes no such code.
- **A code that is taken away is held too.** A region cannot lose its
  code beside a region of its name that says none: the row comes
  under the index when its code goes. It can beside one that says its
  own. One statement cannot take the codes of both regions of a pair
  away. A region that is given its code leaves its name free for one
  without.
- **The index of the old rule found the regions of a country, and the
  new one cannot.** SQLite kept an index for the old rule, over the
  country and then the name, and two tests held that it finds the
  regions of one country: for whoever asks, when a country is to be
  deleted and SQLite looks whether it has regions, and for the two
  triggers of DAT-067 on the countries. An index over some rows only
  cannot serve a question about all rows. So the country gets an
  index of its own, `ix_region_country_id`, and the two tests hold
  that one now.
- **The regions of one name in a country** are looked for among the
  regions of that country, by the index over the country: at most 221
  rows on the standard's list. A region without a code is found by
  its country and name through the index of the rule, where the
  question says that it has none. Tests hold both plans.
- **A statement that stores a region or does nothing where one of its
  name is there** has to say which regions the rule is over: `ON
  CONFLICT (country_id, name) WHERE iso_3166_2 IS NULL DO NOTHING`.
  Without the condition SQLite refuses the statement with "ON
  CONFLICT clause does not match any PRIMARY KEY or UNIQUE
  constraint". No code of the store writes such a statement yet; a
  test holds both, for the task that will. A statement that did
  nothing has used up a key all the same, which the test also holds.
- **The index SQLite keeps is now that of the codes.** The table had
  two rules written into it and SQLite kept an index for each, by
  number: `sqlite_autoindex_region_1` for the names and `_2` for the
  codes. With one rule left, the index of the codes is `_1`. Two tests
  that name it by number name the other number now.
- **The migration builds the table anew**, because SQLite has no
  statement that takes a rule out of a table. It is the seventeenth
  migration's way, with two things more: the regions have codes now,
  which are copied with the rest, and the table has two triggers on
  it since the nineteenth, which go with the table when it is dropped
  and are made again, with the same text. The two triggers on the
  countries are not the table's and stay.
- **The triggers are made after the table is filled.** A store could
  hold a region whose code is not of its country, stored before the
  nineteenth migration, which left it as it was. Made before the
  filling, the trigger for a new region would refuse that row and the
  migration would fail. Made after, it leaves the row as it is, to be
  put right or deleted as DAT-067 says. A test holds it on such a
  store, and a planted defect that leaves that row out is found.
- **No region can be in the way of the new rule.** Every store kept
  the old one, which asked for more: where no two regions of a
  country had one name, no two without a code have.
- **The highest key is kept**, as in the seventeenth migration, so
  that the key of a deleted region is not given again; in a table
  that never gave a key there is none to keep, before and after.
- **The migration fails where something of a name it makes is there
  already**: a table of the name of either copy; and, after the table
  is made anew and filled, an index or a trigger of one of the five
  names. The store is then as it was, at version 19, with the old
  rule in force. The last of the five is the last statement of the
  migration.
- **The test of DAT-067 that runs the nineteenth migration alone**
  said `migrate(store) == [19]` on a store at version 18, which was
  the whole history then. With a twentieth it says
  `migrate(store, MIGRATIONS[:19]) == [19]`: it means the nineteenth,
  and the store it then looks at is one at version 19.
- **The model and the migration agree**: the test of the models and
  the migrations passes unchanged, and planted defects that change
  one of the two alone are found by it.

+have one name: Maputo the city and Maputo the province of Mozambique.
