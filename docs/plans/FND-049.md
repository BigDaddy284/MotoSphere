## Objective

On a narrow screen the top bar is as tall while the data loads as
afterwards, so that nothing under it moves when the data arrives.

## Why

On a phone the top bar shows the data status and the date under its
first line. The two wrap by how long their words are. While the page
says "Loading data…" they fit on one line; once it says when the data
was updated they need two. So the bar grows by a line a moment after
the page has appeared (from 78 to 99 pixels at 360 pixels wide), and
the globe stage under it moves down. A page that moves under the
visitor's finger just as they reach for it is the first thing they see
of MotoSphere on a phone (CLAUDE.md §8). FND-048 found it and made the
tests wait for it; this task takes it away.

## What this task is, and what it is not

- **It is** a change to one style sheet,
  `frontend/src/layout/AppShell.module.css`: on a narrow screen the
  width alone decides how many lines the information has.
- **It is** a new file of browser tests, `frontend/e2e/arrival.spec.ts`
  (37 tests), which measure the page before the data is there and
  afterwards.
- **It is** one changed expectation in a unit test, and two changed
  comments and one changed number in `frontend/e2e/narrow.spec.ts`.
- **It is not** a change to what the top bar says, to any component, or
  to a wide screen.
- **It is not** a change to the first line of the bar (the name between
  the two buttons).

## Design in short

- **Below 640 pixels** each piece of information has a line of its own,
  always: the data status, and under it the date
  (`flex-direction: column`). The bar is 99 pixels tall, whatever the
  words.
- **From 640 pixels on**, up to the wide screen at 900, the two share
  one line, always (`flex-direction: row`, never wrapped). The bar is
  78 pixels tall, whatever the words.
- **A piece too long for its line is not wrapped.** It ends in "…"
  (`overflow: hidden; text-overflow: ellipsis`). On a shared line the
  data status stays whole (`flex: none`) and the date gives way. A data
  status too long for the line by itself ends at the edge of the line
  (`max-width: 100%`). So the height holds for words nobody measured.
- **Why 640.** The planner measured both pieces in each of the 61
  languages the test browser writes dates in, on every day of a year,
  in the font of the bar: the widest pair (Latvian) needs 532 pixels,
  572 with the room between them and the edges of the bar. 640 leaves
  room for wider fonts, and a phone held sideways (640 pixels and more)
  keeps the shorter bar, where height is scarce.
- **The width is written twice**: in the style sheet and in the browser
  tests, which stand one pixel on either side of it. The unit test that
  names every question about width the style sheet asks
  (`narrowScreen.test.tsx`) gets the second question.

## What the task left open, and what the trial settled

- **From which width do the two share a line?** 640 pixels, as above.
  Until now the tests assumed 768, which was only the next width they
  tried; below it the answer depended on the words.
- **What does a failed or slow load show?** The same bar. "Data
  unavailable" and "Reload the page to see the data" are measured at
  every width of the new tests, and a load that is held back is how
  every one of them starts.
- **Is the first line of the bar held to a number at 320 pixels?** No,
  as in the existing tests: there the name may not fit between the
  buttons with every font. At 320 the bar is held to be the same
  before and after, and from 360 on also to its number.
- **Does anything a visitor sees change once the data is there?** No,
  at the widths the existing tests measure. Below 640 pixels the two
  pieces now always have a line each, where words short enough used
  to share one; from 640 on they always share one.
- **Why are the pieces as wide as the line where each has its own?**
  Because nothing needs them narrower, and it is the plainest style
  that holds: a planted defect showed that a rule to keep them as wide
  as their words was held by no test, so it is not there.
